# Tell — Charter

Return to this when deciding what to build, change or cut. A change that can't be justified
here doesn't ship. `AGENTS.md` turns it into rules the code must keep; `DECISIONS.md` records why.

## Promise

**See what would make a choice clearer.**

Tell is a playful choice workshop for people making creative work and independent projects. It
helps you examine a choice; it never makes one.

## The two moments

- **"I keep circling this choice."** Before more research, revision or deliberation: what would
  actually help? Bring the choice, look at the part that is unresolved, and leave with what became
  clearer, what is still open and, if you want, a next move.
- **"Give me something interesting to think through."** A short invented story, two to four
  minutes, with a first answer, a twist or a reveal, a second answer and an ending.

A session should offer a useful distinction, a better alternative, a condition that changes an
answer, or a next move. These are intended benefits, not guaranteed discoveries.

## Who it is for

People making creative work and independent projects: writers, musicians, makers, designers,
developers building their own things. The stories are set in that world.

## One loop

Play or bring a choice → explore the relevant uncertainty → review a concise takeaway →
optionally keep it or apply it → revisit the evidence, or what happened.

## The backbone

Four questions organise both the stories and real choices:

- **Purpose** — what outcome would make this worthwhile?
- **Possibilities** — are these the right options?
- **Priorities** — what matters when good things conflict?
- **Evidence** — what assumption, information or trial could change what you'd do?

## Method, and its limits

The four questions are a light adaptation of two sources, used to choose which questions to ask
and to write the stories:

- Strategic Decisions Group, *Decision Quality*: the right frame, creative alternatives,
  meaningful information, clear values and trade-offs, sound reasoning and commitment to action.
  https://sdg.com/decision-quality/
- Ralph L. Keeney (1996), "Value-focused thinking: Identifying decision opportunities and creating
  alternatives", *European Journal of Operational Research* 92(3), 537–549: start from what you
  want, then create better options. https://doi.org/10.1016/0377-2217(96)00004-5

Tell takes the questions, not the claims. It is not a validated theory of why choices get stuck,
it sorts no one into types, and it scores nothing. It keeps four things apart because each calls
for something different: what matters to you (priorities), lines you won't cross (constraints),
facts you don't know (uncertainty) and things you're taking for granted (assumptions). Some
uncertainty always remains.

**Limits.** Answers to invented stories can differ from real behaviour. The backbone organises a
conversation; it doesn't diagnose which part of a choice is stuck. Matching is simple on purpose,
and two choices with the same shape can differ in everything that matters. A good outcome
doesn't prove good reasoning.

**Untested hypotheses** (what `PILOT.md` checks, without analytics): the stories are enjoyable
enough to play unprompted; a session leaves a real choice clearer; past examples help more than
they distract.

## Principles

1. **The player decides.** Tell asks, structures and remembers; it never recommends.
2. **Answers before interpretations.** What was answered is shown apart from what it might mean,
   and from what stays unknown. Interpretations are offered as possibilities, never findings.
3. **Only what you keep.** Tell never infers a takeaway, a pattern or a trait. You keep, revise
   or retire your own, and the evidence behind each stays intact.
4. **Transparent rules.** Every example Tell offers says why it appeared, by a rule the player
   can read in the app. Tell never pretends to understand free text.
5. **Fair play.** Stories are plainly invented, offer genuinely plausible answers and hide no
   correct philosophy: not always test, always simplify, always keep options open.
6. **Play, not pressure.** A finite library, explicit endings, replay by choice. No streaks,
   scores, reminders, locks or nudges. Coming back is voluntary, and "too early" is an answer.
7. **The player's data, on the player's device.** No accounts, analytics or runtime AI. Export
   and restore always work; plain text is always available.

## The feature test

> Does this help someone see what would make a choice clearer, using only what they told Tell,
> while keeping the record true, the experience light and the choice theirs?

## Scope

**Now (2.0):** a phone PWA with twelve stories (three per question), Bring a choice with drafts,
past examples and voluntary follow-up, a Map of current choices, things worth keeping and the full
history, and local storage with export, restore and plain-text download.
