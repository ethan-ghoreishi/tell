# Tell — Charter

Return to this when deciding what to build, change or cut. A change that can't be justified
here doesn't ship. `AGENTS.md` turns it into rules the code must keep; `DECISIONS.md` records why.

## Vision

People make their own calls, and know what actually matters to them when they choose, as
technology offers to choose more for them.

## Mission

Small choice experiments that show what actually matters to you in a decision, and a record
of your own trade-offs that grows more useful every time you use it.

## Promise

Tell never asks you the hard question. It asks small, easy choices and shows what they add up to.

## The problem

People who make things face many small and medium calls: which tool or gear to buy, whether to
take the gig, what to charge, whether to ship now or polish, keep going or stop. They rarely fail
for lack of information. They go round in circles, research things that wouldn't change the
answer, weigh everything equally, and never find out whether past calls were good. Asked
directly ("what matters most to you?"), people give weak answers; small forced choices show more.

## Core job

Two moments, one practice:

- **Five spare minutes.** A daily set of three experiments, worth playing on its own, that leaves
  a result on your map.
- **A real call.** The same experiments with your own options: what matters, what would switch
  you, which unknowns are worth finding out. Later, how it went.

## Who it is for

First, people who make things: builders, freelancers, designers, developers, product people.
Later, anyone who makes their own calls.

## Principles

1. **Every session earns its keep.** Each experiment ends in a result by construction: a switch
   point, an order, an agreement count, a fact that moved you, or that something didn't matter.
2. **Choices before statements.** Tell asks what you'd choose, never why you behave as you do.
3. **Evidence before interpretation.** Show the counts, with denominators, before anything else.
4. **Transparent rules.** Every pick, result and pattern follows a rule the player can read in the app.
5. **The player has the last word.** Their view of a pattern is a note, never proof.
6. **Light accumulation.** The record grows by itself; nothing to memorise, nothing lost.
7. **Play, not pressure.** Progress means sharper evidence. Short sets with a clear end; no
   streaks, penalties or nudges; the player's data under the player's control.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Answers are recorded facts. Results are fixed sentences filled from those facts.
- No pattern without a published rule. Tell never describes the person, only their choices.
- Comparisons only where one tagged condition differs, and always as a question.
- Invented situations are labelled as invented.
- No runtime AI, accounts, analytics or third-party network. Data stays on the device unless the
  player exports it; shared lessons carry only Tell's fixed vocabulary.

## The feature test

> Does this make a real call clearer, or make five spare minutes worth it, using only what the
> player told Tell, while keeping the record true and the experience light?

## Success

People come back without being reminded; sets are rated useful or interesting; real calls get
brought to Tell and closed with a follow-up.

## Scope

**Now (v2):** a phone PWA with a daily set (Slide, Cut, Spend, Peek), Decide for real calls with
follow-ups, a Map of numbers, patterns, calls, lessons and principles, lessons shared by file,
and local storage with export and restore.

**Later, only if v2 earns it:** see `FUTURE.md`.
