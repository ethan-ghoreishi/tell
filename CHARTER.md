# Tell — Charter

Return to this when deciding what to build, change or cut. A change that can't be justified
here doesn't ship. `AGENTS.md` turns it into rules the code must keep; `DECISIONS.md` records why;
`CONTENT.md` says how rounds are made.

## Vision

**A game of ideas that stay with you.**

Tell turns real events, puzzles, thought experiments, cultural perspectives and strange facts
into short rounds worth playing for themselves. The ideas worth keeping go into a personal
commonplace book, where, over time, they support, challenge, qualify and reinterpret each other.

Play → Discover → Connect → Keep.

## What Tell is

- **Play.** A round takes one to three minutes: a situation, a question, a guess, an order to
  put things in, or a dilemma; then a reveal of what happened, what is true, or how people have
  answered. Some questions have right answers; some expose real disagreement; a round can turn on
  a changed condition or a second example. The form follows the idea, never a formula.
- **Keep.** Each round leaves one idea: a question to ask, a way to think, a saying with its real
  author, an insight. One tap keeps it in the player's words or the round's; moving on costs
  nothing.
- **Connect.** Rounds that bear on each other say how, by hand, in a sentence. When a later round
  supports or challenges an idea already kept, it says so as the round ends; the Book shows each
  idea's connections. Kept ideas come back in play where they're relevant, not on a schedule.
- **The Book** is the cumulative value: ideas with their meaning, their round and their sources,
  retrievable and growing more connected, without journalling or filing.

## Principles

1. **Curiosity carries it.** Content is chosen because it surprises, challenges an assumption or
   connects, not because it fills a topic. Range across science, history, philosophy, art,
   culture, language, economics and practical wisdom.
2. **Trustworthy.** Facts are checked against accessible, reliable sources before they ship, and
   each claim says how sure it is: established, disputed, interpretation, tradition or legend,
   hypothetical. Simplifications are named. No invented proverbs, no attributions without a
   source, no artificial morals.
3. **Effortless.** Open and play. One clear action at a time, familiar controls, calm screens,
   restrained motion. Nothing to set up, file or write.
4. **Play, not pressure.** No scores, streaks, reminders, levels or counts. A finite collection;
   when it's all played, Tell says so and offers it again.
5. **The player is never the subject.** Answers are the player's own; Tell never infers traits,
   beliefs or preferences from them.
6. **The player's data, on the player's device.** No accounts, analytics or runtime AI. Backup,
   restore and plain text always work, and a record from any version is never lost.

## The feature test

> Does this make a round more worth playing, an idea more worth keeping, or the connections
> between ideas easier to find, while keeping the content trustworthy and the experience light?

## Scope

**Now (5.0):** a phone PWA with sixteen rounds in four interaction forms, a Book of kept ideas
with search and editing, hand-written connections between rounds, sources with statuses on every
round, and local storage with backup, restore and plain text.

**Later, only if 5.0 earns it:** see `FUTURE.md`.
