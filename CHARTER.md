# Tell — Charter

Return to this when deciding what to build, change or cut. A change that can't be justified
here doesn't ship. `AGENTS.md` turns it into rules the code must keep; `DECISIONS.md` records why.

## What Tell is

**Small situations to play, and what connects the choices you make in them.**

Tell is a collection of intriguing, playable situations: an old oak over a school path, a
vineyard waiting on a storm, a lighthouse keeper's winter order. Each is a small choice somewhere
different, two or three minutes long, and works on its own. Some share a shape with another set
somewhere quite different. Once both have been played, Tell shows what connects them, what the
player chose in each, and a distinction or question that the two together bring out.

## The hypothesis

Enjoyable situations in different settings can reveal a useful shared structure when compared.

This is a hypothesis Tell exists to test, not a benefit it claims. Two risks matter more than
any technical one: that the situations become predictable, and that the connections feel clever
but not useful. `PILOT.md` tests both.

## The hidden model

Every situation is written against three questions, chosen because together they decide what
finding out is worth and what a choice leaves open:

- **The way back.** Can what you do be undone, and at what price?
- **Finding out.** What can you learn, when, and at what cost: bought before acting, arriving by
  itself while you can still act, only by acting, or only after you've committed?
- **Waiting.** What does waiting do to what's possible: raise the risk, keep options at a price,
  or wear an option away?

Actions, information, consequences and the options that remain, kept to the three questions
that change how the others play out. Money, time, audience size and the like are ingredients
of a story, never things Tell measures a person on. The model is used to write situations and
to state honestly why two are connected; it is not a theory of the player, and nothing is scored.

## Principles

1. **Curiosity carries it.** Interesting choices, not instructions or promises of
   self-improvement. One clear action at a time, familiar controls, concise words, calm screens.
2. **No formula.** Situations vary how they're played (finding things out from a budget,
   comparing plans and seeing what follows, waiting day by day, sharing something scarce,
   discovering another option) and some have no twist at all. Mechanics serve a situation's
   tension, never variety for its own sake.
3. **Fair play.** Several responses are genuinely reasonable, and every ending shows some of
   them. No tricks, no hidden correct philosophy, no lesson disguised as a choice. Staying with
   an answer is as meaningful as changing it.
4. **Connections earn their place.** A connection is authored between two situations that share
   a tension and differ on one question of the model. It must add a distinction or question
   beyond the two answers. Showing no connection is a valid outcome.
5. **Answers, not reasons.** Tell shows what was chosen, never why. Different answers in two
   situations don't show which difference mattered, and Tell says so. It never describes the
   person, infers a trait or a stable preference, or asks anyone to approve, sort or maintain an
   observation.
6. **Play, not pressure.** A finite collection, explicit endings, continuing and replaying by
   choice. No streaks, scores, reminders, locks or nudges.
7. **The player's data, on the player's device.** No accounts, analytics or runtime AI. Backup,
   restore and plain text always work.

## The feature test

> Does this make a situation more worth playing, or a connection more honestly useful, while
> keeping the record true, the experience light and the conclusions the player's own?

## Scope

**Now (3.0):** a phone PWA with six situations in three connected pairs, endings for every path,
a Map of connections and of what was chosen, and local storage with backup, restore and plain
text.
